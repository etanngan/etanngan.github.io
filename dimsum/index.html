Our story

Two generations, one stack of steamers.

Lau Wing-Kuen started in a Guangzhou teahouse at sixteen. He pressed dumpling wrappers with the flat of a cleaver for six years before anyone let him pleat a har gow. In 2009 he and his wife Mei opened Jade Basket in Markham with eleven tables and a single steamer station.

Their daughter Carmen runs the floor now, and Uncle Kuen still stands at the pass every morning. The menu has hardly moved: shrimp peeled in our own kitchen, wrappers made by hand, and nothing goes into the steamer until your order comes in.

“Dad tastes the har gow filling at six, before anyone else is allowed near it. If he frowns, we start the batch again.”

    How yum cha works here

    Tea first. Then the chit.

    1. 壹

      Choose your tea

      Jasmine, pu-erh, tieguanyin, chrysanthemum pu-erh or shou mei. It is $2.00 a person and we refill it all morning. When the pot runs dry, rest the lid ajar and someone will come.

    2. 貳

      Mark the chit

      Write how many of each dish you want on the paper chit and hand it to any server. Order in rounds, three or four baskets at a time. Dim sum is at its best in the first five minutes.

    3. 叁

      Watch for the stamp

      Each basket that lands gets a stamp on your table card by size: small, medium, large, extra-large or special. That card is your bill.

    4. 肆

      Pay at the front

      Take the card to the counter by the door. Cash, debit, Visa and Mastercard. HST is added to all prices.
