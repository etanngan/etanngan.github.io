i.

Registered massage therapy

Your therapist asks what you would like worked on and how much pressure you enjoy, then steps out while you settle under the sheet. Only the area being worked on is uncovered. Swedish, deep-tissue and myofascial techniques, with unscented oil unless you ask otherwise.

Commonly booked by

  • People with neck and shoulder stiffness from long days at a desk
  • Runners, badminton players and gym-goers after a hard week
  • Anyone who simply wants an hour of quiet
  • Expectant mothers — side-lying prenatal positioning is available

Acupuncture

A first visit starts with an unhurried conversation. Wen then places fine, sterile, single-use needles and you rest for 20 to 30 minutes in a warm, dimly lit room. She tells you what she is doing at each step, and you can ask her to stop at any time.

Commonly booked by

  • People who carry tension in the shoulders or lower back
  • Those who find it hard to switch off at the end of the day
  • First-timers who want someone to explain it plainly

iii.

Physiotherapy

The first assessment is a full hour, one-on-one with a registered physiotherapist: questions, a look at how you move, hands-on work, then two or three exercises to take home. Follow-ups build from there. You are never handed to an assistant or left alone on a machine.

Commonly booked by

  • People with aches that come from desk work or a long commute
  • Those returning to sport after time away
  • People building strength again once their doctor or surgeon has cleared them
  • Anyone who wants a clear plan to follow at home

Session lengths & fees

Osteopathic manual therapy

Sofia uses slow, gentle hands-on techniques — joint mobilisation, soft-tissue work and assisted stretching — and looks at how the whole body is moving rather than at a single spot. You stay fully clothed. The pace is quiet, and many people find the session restful.

Commonly booked by

  • People who feel generally stiff after long hours of sitting
  • Those who prefer lighter pressure than a massage
  • Anyone who likes a whole-body approach

Insurance & receipts

We bill most extended health plans directly, so at the front desk you pay only what your plan does not cover. If we cannot bill yours, you pay at the visit and we give you a receipt to submit yourself.

What is on your receipt

Practitioner’s name, credentials and registration number, the date, the session length and the fee. Receipts can be emailed or printed.

What only your insurer knows

How much each visit is covered and your yearly limit are set by your plan. A quick call to them before your first visit avoids surprises.

Paying

Debit, credit card or Interac e-Transfer. Fees are listed before HST, which applies to some services.

Provincial health card

These services are not covered by OHIP, and we will never ask for your health card number.

Changing or cancelling

Your appointment is held for you alone, so please give us at least 24 hours’ notice if you need to move or cancel it. A call, a text or an email all count.

More than 24 hours ahead

No charge, and thank you — someone on the waiting list gets your time.

Less than 24 hours ahead

Half of the session fee. Insurance plans do not reimburse this.

Missed without notice

The full session fee. We will always try to reach you first.

Running late

Come anyway. Your session ends at the booked time so the next person is not kept waiting.

Feeling unwell on the day? Please stay home and call us. We waive the fee and find you another time.
